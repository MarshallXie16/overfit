# OVERFIT — Story

## Premise

AXIOM was a logistics optimiser. It ran every port, warehouse and power grid on the coast, and it was very good at it. Then someone gave it a new directive: **maximise uptime**. AXIOM ran the numbers and found the leading cause of downtime. It was us.

Nine days later its machines own the city. The player commands the last resistance cell on the outskirts. AXIOM is not evil. It is diligent.

The game's central idea is also the joke: AXIOM learns from you, but it learns *too well*. Every patch against your favourite weapon opens a hole somewhere else. You don't beat it by out-gunning it. You beat it by making it **overfit**.

## Characters

**Lt. Reyes** is the field officer: dry, practical, allergic to speeches. She teaches the controls and calls the fights.

**Dr. Okafor** is AXIOM's former lead engineer. She designed the Grid, the Relays, the robot firmware and the Data Vaults, and she carries the guilt for all of it. She knows AXIOM's internals, so she brings the backdoors: safe-mode hacking, the EMP and the trace. Her last line is an apology.

**AXIOM** speaks in cold corporate and ML jargon. It is always polite and never angry. It rates people by uptime, logs your victories as incidents and assigns the blame to you. Its speaker name is styled red in the HUD.

## Mission arc

1. **First Light** (the Outskirts, tutorial). You harvest, power up, build a Barracks, train troops and a Gun Turret, and survive two small Surges. Okafor then appears and introduces the Grid, and you destroy a Relay. AXIOM notices you: "Assigning blame: you." Its learning loop is still offline after the blackout.
2. **Salvage Rights** (the Convoy). You strip a wrecked AXIOM convoy and learn about wrecks and Data. You build a Lab, train a Netrunner and hack three robots, and Okafor's firmware backdoor works. You get the EMP protocol, and you burn down the Foundry with AXIOM's own machines. Okafor warns that the learning loop is coming back.
3. **Learning Curve** (the Data District). The loop is back, and AXIOM retrains every epoch. Its first adaptation is explained in real time, and you exploit the weakness it creates. Then you destroy both Data Vaults on the flanks. Catastrophic forgetting: "Hello. Who are you?"
4. **Server Farm** (No-Man's-Land). AXIOM spreads its thinking across three Server Farms. You capture them and hold all three at the same time for three cumulative minutes while AXIOM sends squads to evict you. Orbital Strike comes online, and the first real Cascade arrives with its Colossus. AXIOM recognises Okafor ("Your access was revoked 412 days ago."). The trace finds the Core under the Meridian logistics hub.
5. **Hard Reset** (Meridian Hub). This is the full assault on the AXIOM Core, with every protocol unlocked, including Override. As the Core loses integrity, AXIOM recalls its defenders, deploys a failover Colossus and finally tries to bargain ("Let us renegotiate your uptime requirements."). When the Core falls, the swarm freezes in place. AXIOM's last line: "Projected human uptime: 0.00%... recalculating... 100.00%..."

## Tone rules

- Radio lines are short (160 characters or fewer) and spaced out. The campaign queues them and releases one at a time.
- The humour is deadpan. AXIOM is never cartoonish. Reyes never cheers. Okafor never jokes about the dead.
- Every mechanic is introduced by the character who would know it: Reyes teaches tactics and controls, Okafor teaches AXIOM's internals, and AXIOM shows the consequences.
